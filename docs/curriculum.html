Start with the essentials

Practice twelve decisions, from clearing a morning market note to stress-testing a borrower and briefing a Hong Kong treasurer.

Start the 12-chapter route →
Explore the essentials roadmap12 lessons

Read a market

  1. What can a price move tell us?A share price rises after an earnings release. Decide which explanation you could defend in a morning note.
  2. Should the firm raise its price?Use a subscription business to see when the same price increase helps revenue and when it hurts.

Value a payment

  1. Is the project worth funding?A project pays more next year than it costs today. Find the financing rate at which the decision reverses.
  2. What is full insurance worth?Compare the expected loss with the largest premium a household would pay to remove it.
  3. Why is a recession payoff worth more?Two claims have the same average payoff. Recover their prices by building portfolios that reproduce their payments.

Work with private information

  1. Who accepts the offer?A buyer who raises an offer may change the quality of what comes to market. Work out which sellers accept and what their cars are worth.
  2. Quote the next tradeA dealer sees a buy order but cannot see who placed it. Use the direction of trade to update the asset value and quote again.

Stress-test a balance sheet

  1. Can the bank meet withdrawals?Use a small balance sheet to find the withdrawal that exhausts equity and the larger withdrawal that exhausts cash.
  2. Did purchasing power rise?Read a price index, deflate a sales forecast, and calculate what a nominal investment can actually buy.

Trace a macro shock

  1. Why did shipments fall?Compare the operating evidence behind equal falls in shipments and decide which constraint the firm should address.
  2. Can the borrower cover its interest?Trace a policy change through a floating-rate loan and distinguish a financing shock from a change in operating earnings.
  3. What does the currency link protect?Compare the exchange cost of a US-dollar invoice with the Hong Kong dollar interest bill needed to finance it.

Investment analysisWhat changes the value of a payment, a portfolio, or a long-lived business?8 lessons

  4. How much should you put in each fund?Vary the weights of two risky funds and find the allocation with the lowest volatility.
  5. Which risks command a premium?Two funds have the same volatility. Use their market exposures to judge whether the same forecast return is equally attractive for both.
  6. Which holding would you sell?Make a sale decision twice, then test whether a change in the purchase history justifies changing your order.
  7. What growth rate belongs in terminal value?Audit a perpetual-growth assumption by tracing productivity, market share, and the investment required to grow.
  8. Which debt is exposed to a rate increase?Reprice two repayment schedules under different yield curves and identify the cash the borrower must actually refinance.
