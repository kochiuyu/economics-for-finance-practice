Chapter 23 · Demand and supply shocks

Why did shipments fall?

Compare the operating evidence behind equal falls in shipments and decide which constraint the firm should address.

Your decision. Two manufacturers each shipped 100 units last month and 80 this month. One wants a sales campaign. The other wants to qualify an alternative supplier. Which response fits each firm?

Read prices, orders, and capacity together

Use these illustrative operating records. An order is net of cancellations. Unfilled orders carry forward. Both firms started the month with an empty order book. There is no finished inventory.

Operating measure Firm A Firm B
Previous price, HK$ per unit 100 100
Current price 95 110
New orders 80 115
Maximum units producible this month 110 80
Units shipped 80 80
Orders carried forward 0 35

Firm A has idle capacity, fewer orders, and a lower price. A fall in demand fits the joint pattern. Firm B has a full plant at its reduced capacity, a backlog, and a higher price. A loss of supply fits this evidence. In a simple market diagram, a leftward demand shift lowers both quantity and price. A leftward supply shift lowers quantity and raises price. Administered prices, inventories, and simultaneous shifts make the price sign alone a weak diagnostic.

Calculate the cash consequences

Assume customers pay on shipment. Cash receipts are HK$7,600 at A and HK$8,800 at B, compared with HK$10,000 before. Suppose A’s variable cost stays at HK$60 per unit, while emergency sourcing raises B’s to HK$80. Contribution falls from HK$4,000 at each firm to HK$2,800 at A and HK$2,400 at B. B commands a higher price yet loses more contribution.

Which intervention would you fund?

At A, bringing in ten additional orders at the current price adds 10 × (95 − 60) = HK$350 before campaign cost. At B, ten additional units of usable capacity add 10 × (110 − 80) = HK$300 before qualification cost, provided the backlog holds. More demand at B initially enlarges the queue. More capacity at A initially sits idle. Compare the incremental contribution with the price of the proposed intervention.

Take a firm diagnosis to a macro case

A firm can lose demand while the economy faces a supply disruption. Ask which bottleneck binds at the firm, what other sectors are reporting, and whether prices adjust within the period. A fall in total output is compatible with more than one mix of shocks. The case asks you to choose what to finance when both shocks occur together.
