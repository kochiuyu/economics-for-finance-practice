Chapter 22 · Productivity and valuation

What growth rate belongs in terminal value?

Audit a perpetual-growth assumption by tracing productivity, market share, and the investment required to grow.

Your decision. An analyst raises a mature supplier’s perpetual nominal cash-flow growth forecast from 3% to 5%. The justification is a new machine that will increase output per worker by 10% next year. Would you approve the higher terminal value?

Distinguish a level gain from a growth rate

Ten workers each produce 100 units a year. After installing the machine, each produces 110. Output rises from 1,000 to 1,100. If staffing and productivity then stay fixed, the next year’s output is still 1,100. The extra 100 units recur. The 10% growth rate occurs once. Put the installation cost and the higher subsequent operating cash flows into the dated forecast.

For a longer-run comparison, suppose labor input grows 1% annually and output per unit of labor grows 2%. Real output grows 1.01 × 1.02 − 1 = 3.02%. If selling prices rise by 2% annually, nominal sales grow about 5.08%, assuming the firm keeps its share and product mix. Extending that sales figure into cash-flow growth requires assumptions about margins and reinvestment. Economy-wide productivity alone cannot supply them.

Price the assumption

At the end of year five, assume year-six free cash flow is HK$100 million and the nominal discount rate is 8%. All later cash flows grow at a constant nominal rate. Terminal value at year five equals year-six cash flow divided by the difference between the discount rate and growth. The discount rate must exceed perpetual growth.

Growth after year six Year-five terminal value Value today, discounted five years
2% HK$1,667m HK$1,134m
3% HK$2,000m HK$1,361m
5% HK$3,333m HK$2,269m

Holding the first cash flow fixed isolates sensitivity to growth. Moving from 3% to 5% adds roughly HK$908 million today. A valuation of the whole firm would also include the first five years of cash flows.

Make growth pay for itself

Suppose instead that year-six after-tax operating profit is fixed at HK$125 million. Each dollar reinvested earns a sustained 15% annual return. In steady state, growing at 3% requires reinvesting 3/15 = 20% of profit. That leaves HK$100 million of free cash flow, as above. At 5% growth, can the analyst still distribute HK$100 million?

Rebuild the year-six cash flow

Reinvestment rises to one-third of profit, or HK$41.67 million. Free cash flow falls to HK$83.33 million. Terminal value at year five becomes 83.33 / 0.03 ≈ HK$2,778 million. Faster growth still creates value here because new capital earns more than its 8% cost. Holding the original cash flow fixed overstated the benefit.

Decision. Incorporate the machine’s level gain once its cost and useful life are established. Keep the perpetual growth assumption separate until the analyst can explain continuing productivity gains, competitive entry, and the return on the capital needed to sustain them.
