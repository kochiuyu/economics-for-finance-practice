Chapter 21 · Measurement and inflation

Did purchasing power rise?

Read a price index, deflate a sales forecast, and calculate what a nominal investment can actually buy.

Your decision. A retailer reports that sales increased from HK$100 million to HK$108 million. Its product-price index rose from 100 to 106. Can the analyst describe the 8% sales increase as volume growth?

Reprice the current basket at base-period prices

Assume the price index measures this retailer’s products and that product mix and quality are unchanged. Divide current sales by 106 / 100. That gives HK$101.89 million in base-period prices, up 1.89%. Multiplying that real growth factor by the price factor reconstructs the cash receipts.

Measure Base year Current year
Nominal sales, HK$ million 100 108
Price index, base year = 100 100 106
Sales at base-year prices, HK$ million 100 101.89

Subtracting 6% from 8% gives a convenient approximation of 2%. The exact calculation is 1.08 / 1.06 − 1. State which index you used. A consumer price index tracks a household basket and could give a misleading volume estimate for a specialist retailer.

Test the purchasing power of a deposit

A household places HK$10,000 in a one-year deposit paying 4%. A basket costs HK$100 today and HK$106 when the deposit matures. Today the household could buy 100 baskets. The maturity balance buys 10,400 / 106 = 98.11 baskets. The realized real return is 1.04 / 1.06 − 1 = −1.89%.

What return could the household reasonably expect at the start?

If the household forecast 2% inflation with certainty, its projected real return was 1.04 / 1.02 − 1 = 1.96%. The 6% inflation realization explains the shortfall in purchasing power. Use the inflation assumption available when the investment was made to assess the original decision.

Carry the distinction into a macro forecast

GDP measures domestic value added over a period. A year-end stock of inventories and a year’s production have different units of time. When using GDP to justify a sales forecast, first identify the economy, period, and price basis. Then ask whether the retailer serves the same customers and sells the same mix as the aggregate. A macro volume forecast of 2% is a starting comparison. Moving it into a firm forecast requires a view on the firm’s market share and prices.

Compare your recommendation

Report cash sales growth of 8% and estimated volume growth of 1.89% under the retailer’s price index. Check whether product mix changed before interpreting the estimate as growth in units sold.

Translate a real return target into a deposit quote

To deliver 2% real growth with 6% inflation, the account must turn HK$1 into enough cash to buy 1.02 times the original basket. The nominal gross return is 1.02 × 1.06 = 1.0812. A quote of 8.12% meets the target in this scenario. Repeat the calculation for the inflation rate you would stress, using the spending basket that matters to the depositor.
