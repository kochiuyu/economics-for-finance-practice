Chapter 5 · Intertemporal choice

Is the project worth funding?

A project pays more next year than it costs today. Find the financing rate at which the decision reverses.

Your decision. A firm can pay HK$100,000 today to receive a certain HK$108,000 exactly one year from now. It has to borrow the full cost. Should it accept a one-year loan at 6%? What would you say at 10%?

Put both choices on the same date

At 6% the firm owes HK$106,000 at maturity. The project leaves HK$2,000 after repayment. In today’s money that surplus is 2,000 / 1.06 = HK$1,886.79. Equivalently, subtract the initial outlay from the present value of the receipt. The two methods agree because they compare the same cash flows on a common date.

Rate Loan repayment in one year Surplus at maturity Net present value today
6% 106,000 +2,000 +1,886.79
8% 108,000 0 0
10% 110,000 −2,000 −1,818.18

The switch occurs at 108,000 / 100,000 − 1 = 8%. This comparison assumes certain payment, no fees or taxes, and a single drawdown with one repayment. If the receipt were risky, its promised amount and a borrowing quote alone would be insufficient to value the project.

Read the household budget line

Suppose a household starts with 100 units of income today and 50 next year. It can lend or borrow at 6%. If it consumes 80 today, it saves 20 and can consume 50 + 20 × 1.06 = 71.2 next year. If it consumes 120 today, it borrows 20 and has 28.8 left next year. Each extra unit consumed today costs 1.06 units next year. Preferences determine which affordable pair the household chooses.

What if saving earns 4% while borrowing costs 10%?

The budget line bends where consumption today equals current income. Consuming 80 today now leaves 50 + 20 × 1.04 = 70.8 next year. Consuming 120 leaves 50 − 20 × 1.10 = 28. A firm with HK$100,000 idle that would otherwise earn 4% gains HK$4,000 at maturity from the project. A firm financing the same project entirely at 10% loses HK$2,000. The source of the marginal dollar changes the decision.

Make the financing recommendation

At 6%, accept under the stated assumptions. At 10%, decline. If a lender also charges an up-front fee, deduct it from today’s net present value. A HK$2,000 fee paid from cash today would turn the 6% decision negative. This is why an analyst asks for the drawdown, fee, and repayment dates before comparing two term sheets.
