Chapter 27

How many US dollars should the importer hedge?

Match the size and settlement date of a hedge to the net dollar payment.

Worked example Lab Application

Your decision. Take the baseline 90-day US$1 million invoice. A US$300,000 customer receipt is certain on the same date. The treasury team can buy dollars forward at HK$7.81 per US dollar. Should it buy the full million?

Offset same-day receipts before booking the forward

The receipt can settle part of the invoice. Net exposure is US$700,000. At settlement spot rates of 7.75 and 7.85, buying the residual costs HK$5,425,000 or HK$5,495,000. A forward purchase of 700,000 fixes that outlay at HK$5,467,000. These are illustrative treasury quotes with no fees or collateral calls.

A million-dollar forward costs HK$7,810,000. After paying the invoice, the firm has the customer’s US$300,000 left to sell at spot. Net HK-dollar outlay is therefore HK$5,485,000 at 7.75 or HK$5,455,000 at 7.85. Hedging the gross invoice reverses the direction of the residual exposure.

Resize the hedge when the invoice changes

What changes if the supplier reduces its invoice to US$960,000 under the tariff scenario?

Work through the decision

Assuming the same US$300,000 receipt, the net invoice exposure becomes US$660,000. The previous 700,000 forward overhedges by 40,000. Track any duty payable separately by currency and settlement date. If the receipt arrives late, a matched net hedge still leaves a temporary dollar funding need.
