Chapter 29

Can the importer survive a failed rollover?

Separate the dollar amount coming due from the amount the bank agrees to refinance.

Your decision. The importer financed its baseline US$1 million invoice with a dollar loan. At maturity it has the US$300,000 customer receipt and HK$2 million in unrestricted cash. How much of the loan must its bank renew?

Build the comparison

Assume interest has already been paid. A renewed dollar of principal removes one dollar from the amount due today and creates a future obligation. At 7.80, HK$2 million buys US$256,410. Together with receipts, this covers US$556,410. Minimum rollover is approximately US$443,590.

If the bank renews 500,000, the importer buys 200,000 dollars and spends HK$1.56 million. With renewal cut to 400,000 and spot at 7.85, it must buy 300,000 dollars for HK$2.355 million. The cash deficit is HK$355,000. This is a funding stress scenario, independent of whether the business will eventually collect additional revenue.

Change an assumption

Would a forward at 7.81 for the required US$300,000 eliminate the deficit in the stressed scenario?

Work through the decision

It fixes the HK-dollar payment at 2.343 million, leaving a deficit of 343,000. Treasury must also arrange funding or accelerate collections. A foreign lender cutting exposure can transmit a broader capital outflow into a single firm’s maturity schedule. To interpret a country’s aggregate flow, distinguish repayment of old claims from issuance of new ones.
