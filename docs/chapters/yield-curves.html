Chapter 25 · Fixed income

Which debt is exposed to a rate increase?

Reprice two repayment schedules under different yield curves and identify the cash the borrower must actually refinance.

Your decision. A treasury team has two fixed obligations, each promising HK$100 million once. One matures in a year and the other in five years. The CFO asks which is more vulnerable to rising rates. Are you preparing a market-value report or next year’s funding budget?

Discount each payment at its maturity

Use annual effective spot rates, which price a single payment at its maturity. Assume certain payment and no credit spread. Initially the curve is flat at 4%. A parallel shift raises every spot rate to 5%. In the steepening scenario the one-year rate stays at 4% and the five-year rate rises to 6%.

Scenario One-year obligation today Five-year obligation today
Flat at 4% HK$96.15m HK$82.19m
Parallel rise to 5% HK$95.24m HK$78.35m
Steepening to 4% and 6% HK$96.15m HK$74.73m

The five-year price under steepening is 100 / 1.06⁵. A portfolio with several payments needs a rate for each payment date. A change in a single headline rate cannot determine its exact price change under a twist in the curve.

Use duration for a quick check

A zero-coupon claim has Macaulay duration equal to its maturity. Its modified duration at a 4% annual yield is maturity / 1.04. For the five-year claim, that gives 4.81. A one-percentage-point parallel increase predicts about a 4.81% price fall. Exact repricing gives 78.35 / 82.19 − 1 ≈ −4.67%. Duration uses the slope at the starting yield. Curvature explains the approximation error. The one-year claim has much less price sensitivity because its payment arrives sooner.

Build next year’s funding budget

Assume each obligation stays outstanding until maturity. There are no coupons. A rise in market yields reduces the value of each liability today, but the issuer still owes the contracted HK$100 million at maturity. The five-year issuer needs no principal refinancing next year. The one-year issuer must repay or roll over the full HK$100 million.

Compare two renewal offers

At maturity the one-year issuer can borrow HK$100 million for another year at either 4% or 6% in two hypothetical funding scenarios. The following year’s interest bill is HK$4 million or HK$6 million. This HK$2 million difference is a future cash cost. Today’s price change in the old liability is a separate calculation. Today’s spot rates alone do not tell us which renewal offer will be available a year from now.

Committee answer. Flag the five-year claim for market-value sensitivity and the one-year claim for near-term funding risk. For an actual issuer, request the maturity ladder, floating-rate reset dates, available cash, and the spread lenders would charge at renewal.
