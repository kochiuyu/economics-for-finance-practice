Chapter 20

Which payment actually leaves more cash?

Evaluate fees, settlement delays, and redemption risk on the same date.

Your decision. A merchant sells goods for HK$100,000. Service A settles in bank money today with a 1.5% fee. Service B charges 1% and delivers a redeemable digital balance in 30 days. Which offer has higher value today?

Build the comparison

A delivers HK$98,500 today. Assume the merchant values cash at simple annual interest of 6% on a 360-day basis. If B pays HK$99,000 at par on day 30, its value today is 99,000 / 1.005 = HK$98,507.46. The fee advantage just covers the wait.

Suppose that in 1% of cases the redemption pays only 90% of the balance, and otherwise pays in full. Expected redemption becomes 99,000 × 0.999 = HK$98,901. Discounting gives HK$98,408.96. A risk-neutral merchant using this forecast prefers A. A merchant with payroll due tomorrow must also check whether it can borrow against the balance.

Change an assumption

How likely can the 10% redemption loss be before A becomes preferable?

Work through the decision

Set 99,000 × (1 − 0.1p) = 98,500 × 1.005. The break-even probability is approximately 0.076%. Ask who owes redemption, when settlement is final, and what cash is available during a delay. The probabilities and terms are illustrative and describe no particular payment instrument.
