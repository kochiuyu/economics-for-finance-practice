Chapter 15 · Market microstructure

Quote the next trade

A dealer sees a buy order but cannot see who placed it. Use the direction of trade to update the asset value and quote again.

Your decision. A security pays 0 or 1 at the end of trading. The two outcomes are equally likely. Half the traders know the payoff. After one buy, what bid and ask should you post for the next order?

A dealer buys at the bid when a trader sells and sells at the ask when a trader buys. An informed trader always buys when the payoff is 1 and sells when it is 0. Each uninformed trader is equally likely to buy or sell. Trader types are independently drawn across orders and independent of the payoff. Competition makes the risk-neutral dealer break even on each side. There are no inventory or processing costs in this exercise.

Why does a buy carry information?

Imagine 100 possible first trades. The high and low states each account for 50. Within each state, 25 traders are informed and 25 are uninformed. The table gives expected counts, so half units are meaningful.

True payoff Informed buys Uninformed buys All buys All sells
1 (high) 25 12.5 37.5 12.5
0 (low) 0 12.5 12.5 37.5
Total 25 25 50 50

Of the 50 buys, 37.5 occur in the high state. A dealer selling into the first buy therefore delivers an asset with expected payoff 37.5/50 = 0.75. This is the opening ask. A dealer buying into a sell receives an asset with expected payoff 12.5/50 = 0.25, the opening bid.

Work out the second quote

After the buy, the probability of the high state is 0.75. A further buy is three times as likely if the payoff is high, so prior odds of 3 to 1 become 9 to 1. A sell supplies the opposite evidence and returns the odds to 1 to 1.

Orders already observed Belief that payoff is 1 Bid for next sell Ask for next buy
None 0.50 0.25 0.75
Buy 0.75 0.50 0.90
Buy, then sell 0.50 0.25 0.75

The first buyer pays 0.75. The 0.90 ask applies to a subsequent buy. That timing distinction is what allows a reader to reconstruct the desk’s trade history.

Derive the quotes for any informed-trader share

Write p for the current probability of the high payoff and α for the informed share. A buy has probability h = (1 + α)/2 in the high state and l = (1 − α)/2 in the low state. Bayes’ rule gives the ask as ph / [ph + (1 − p)l]. For a sell, interchange h and l. The bid is pl / [pl + (1 − p)h]. The quote on the executed side becomes the next belief because the end payoff is either 0 or 1.

Test the mechanism

Set the informed share to zero and try two buys. Each order is now equally likely in either state. Both quotes stay at 0.50. Restore the share to 50% and compare a buy followed by a sell with two consecutive buys. Which history would persuade you more that the asset is valuable?

An actual desk may also charge for inventory risk or processing. To assess whether asymmetric information helps explain a spread, look at whether order direction predicts subsequent changes in value and whether the changes persist.
