Chapter 2 · Demand and substitution

Should the firm raise its price?

Use a subscription business to see when the same price increase helps revenue and when it hurts.

Your decision. A market-data vendor charges HK$100 per month and has 1,000 subscribers. Should it charge HK$110? Give the sales director a break-even subscriber count before looking at the demand forecast.

Find the quantity the firm can afford to lose

Monthly revenue is price times subscribers. The current plan brings in HK$100,000. At HK$110 the firm needs 100,000 / 110 = 909.09 subscribers to match that figure. Subscribers come in whole numbers, so 910 is the smallest count that raises revenue. All figures in this exercise are illustrative.

Monthly price Existing alternatives A close rival offers an easy switch
HK$100 1,000 subscribers 1,000 subscribers
HK$110 940 subscribers 860 subscribers
Revenue at HK$110 HK$103,400 HK$94,600

The rival changes what customers can do when the price rises. With the existing alternatives, revenue rises HK$3,400. With the close rival, it falls HK$5,400. The question for the sales team is how many customers have a credible substitute at the time of renewal.

Measure the response in comparable units

Price elasticity of demand is the percentage change in quantity divided by the percentage change in price. For two observed points, use the average of the two quantities and the average of the two prices as the bases. This midpoint calculation gives the same elasticity in either direction.

Market Quantity change / average quantity Price change / average price Elasticity
Existing alternatives −60 / 970 10 / 105 −0.65
Close rival −140 / 930 10 / 105 −1.58

In the first market, the proportionate gain in price exceeds the loss in quantity. In the second, the quantity response dominates. The threshold magnitude of one separates the two revenue outcomes under this midpoint calculation.

Does the recommendation survive a margin check?

Serving a subscriber costs HK$40 per month. Fixed costs stay the same. Compare contribution, defined here as (price less variable cost) times subscribers. Would you still raise the price when the rival is present?

Check revenue against contribution

At HK$100, contribution is 60 × 1,000 = HK$60,000. At HK$110 with the rival present it is 70 × 860 = HK$60,200. Revenue falls, yet monthly contribution rises by HK$200. Three additional lost subscribers would reverse that decision. Ask how precisely the sales team can estimate churn and whether departing customers are costly to win back.

Connect the demand schedule to consumer choice

A customer chooses among bundles within a budget. A higher own price makes alternatives relatively cheaper and reduces what the same income buys. The Slutsky decomposition separates the substitution response at compensated purchasing power from the income response. A renewal forecast gives the total response. Separating its components would require further information about preferences or income.

What subscriber count should trigger a pricing review?

Compare your recommendation

At HK$110, each subscriber contributes HK$70 after service cost. The old plan earned HK$60,000 in monthly contribution, so the new price needs at least 858 renewals (60,000 ÷ 70, rounded up). Revenue needs 910 renewals to exceed its old level. At the forecast of 860, the contribution gain is only HK$200 a month. Three fewer renewals would erase it. Track renewals and any additional retention spending before extending the price change.
